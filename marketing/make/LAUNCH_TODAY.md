# Lians Make launch today

Date: 2026-08-21

## The one-line promise

> Describe what your group needs. Get a working app you can use and share.

The public action is one word:

> **Make.**

## What is launching

People can make a challenge, vote, quiz, leaderboard, tracker, or shared list from one sentence. Every result is live, stores shared activity, works without participant accounts, and can be shared or remixed.

This is narrower than Bolt, Lovable, Codex, or Claude. Lians does not ask the user to edit code or manage a project. It gives a group the finished place where the activity happens.

## Why this can spread

The product carries its own invitation. A creator only gets value when other people open the link and participate. Every participant sees the Lians mark and a direct path to make their own version.

That is the distribution loop:

```text
Make -> Share -> Join -> See Lians -> Remix -> Share
```

Canva made creation approachable through templates and increasingly treats websites as interactive, shareable surfaces. Canva reports more than [89 million websites created](https://www.canva.com/newsroom/news/canva-websites/). Duolingo documents how its characters and social-first voice became a distribution system, including its [TikTok strategy](https://blog.duolingo.com/duolingo-tiktok/) and [character-led brand work](https://blog.duolingo.com/duolingo-designing-world-class-characters/). Perplexity grew around one plain behavior, answers with sources, before expanding the surface area. Bolt and Lovable showed the demand for prompt-to-result creation, with Lovable reporting [100,000 new projects per day](https://lovable.dev/blog/one-year-of-lovable).

These examples validate patterns, not demand for Lians. The launch exists to measure Lians demand directly.

## First 100 users

Do not launch to people who merely like AI. Launch around a moment they already want to organize.

Start with ten seeds:

1. a friend group choosing a trip;
2. a class running a reading challenge;
3. a gym group tracking thirty days;
4. a birthday host making a quiz;
5. roommates keeping one shared shopping list;
6. a club voting on its next event;
7. a sports group keeping a leaderboard;
8. coworkers running a hydration challenge;
9. a family planning a holiday; and
10. a creator asking followers to choose the next post.

For each seed, make the app with the person live. Ask them to share it while you are still together. The second person matters more than the first signup.

## Launch post

```text
I kept asking what AI should do for normal people.

Not write another answer. Not give you code. Finish something.

So we made Lians.

Describe what your group needs. Lians makes the working app and gives you one link to share.

Try: “Make a 30-day reading challenge for my friends.”

Free while we learn what people make.
```

## Three short-form demos

### Demo 1: The challenge

Hook: “Your group chat will not keep everyone consistent.”

Show the sentence being typed. Show the created challenge. Tap one check-in. Share the link. End on another person's name appearing.

Spoken line: “I typed one sentence. Lians made the challenge, tracked everyone, and gave me the link. No setup.”

### Demo 2: The vote

Hook: “Stop deciding trips with 200 messages.”

Show the prompt, the three choices, two votes arriving, and the winning option.

Spoken line: “Tell Lians what the group needs. It makes the place where the decision happens.”

### Demo 3: Not a chatbot

Hook: “ChatGPT can write the idea. This gives you the result.”

Split the screen. On one side, show text instructions. On the other, show a working shared list where two people add and complete items.

Spoken line: “The difference is the second person. They do not read an answer. They use the thing.”

Keep every demo between 12 and 20 seconds. The result should appear within the first 4 seconds.

## Brand voice

- Say “make,” not “generate.”
- Say “your people,” not “collaborators.”
- Say “one link,” not “deployment.”
- Show an outcome before explaining technology.
- Be playful about group chaos, never about user confusion.
- Use the lotus as a signature, not decoration on every section.

## 72-hour plan

### Hours 0 to 12

- publish the product;
- post Demo 1 on TikTok, Reels, Shorts, and X;
- personally make the first ten apps with real people;
- record every prompt that does not map cleanly; and
- fix only blockers between creation and the second participant.

### Hours 12 to 36

- post Demo 2;
- publish six plain use-case pages based on actual prompts;
- ask each activated creator to invite one more group;
- message every creator whose app has no participant; and
- turn the strongest real app into a public example.

### Hours 36 to 72

- post Demo 3;
- add the two most repeated app requests if they fit the safe schema;
- contact 30 small community organizers with one finished example each;
- publish the first usage numbers without combining views and users; and
- decide whether the share loop is strong enough for another week.

## Use-case pages

Create only pages that match working product shapes:

- `/make/30-day-challenge`
- `/make/group-trip-vote`
- `/make/birthday-quiz`
- `/make/shared-packing-list`
- `/make/friend-leaderboard`
- `/make/group-habit-tracker`

Each page should show the final app first, one example prompt, one large button, and two real remixes. Do not publish empty SEO pages.

## Metrics

The north star is activated apps per week.

An app is activated when someone other than the creator performs a meaningful action.

Track:

- prompts submitted;
- apps created;
- creation completion rate;
- time from prompt to live app;
- apps with a second participant;
- share actions per created app;
- participants per activated app;
- remixes per activated app;
- creators who make a second app within seven days; and
- prompts that the constrained generator cannot satisfy.

Initial gates:

- at least 70 percent of valid prompts create an app;
- at least 35 percent of created apps get a second participant;
- at least 20 percent of activated apps produce a share or remix;
- at least 15 percent of creators make another app within seven days; and
- fewer than 10 percent of sessions need manual help.

These gates are hypotheses. Do not call them benchmarks until real cohorts exist.

## Referral test

After a second person acts, show the creator one prompt:

```text
Your app is alive. Who else needs one?
```

Give two actions: share this app, or make another. Do not add points, coupons, or paid rewards yet.

## Explicit non-goals

- charging before repeat use;
- building arbitrary software;
- user accounts for participants;
- an open-ended chat thread;
- code export;
- custom domains;
- payments;
- enterprise teams;
- a marketplace;
- adding every design tool from the inventory; and
- claiming Lians is a 10 out of 10 business before the loop is measured.

## Stop rule

After 100 created apps, narrow or stop if fewer than 20 receive a real second participant, if normal chat tools satisfy the same job without the shared result, or if most prompts require manual custom development.

Continue if the strongest app shape repeatedly moves from creator to participant to remix with little or no support. That shape becomes the next focused wedge.
