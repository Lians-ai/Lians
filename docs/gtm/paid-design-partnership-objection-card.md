# Paid design-partnership objection card

Use these responses in live buyer conversations. Keep the tone direct and
curious. Do not argue a prospect into a deal that has no workflow, owner, or
budget.

## “Can we start with a free pilot?”

> We keep the first engagement small, but it is paid because it produces a
> defined evidence path, reconstruction, quality findings, impact map, receipt,
> and implementation report. If the problem is not worth $4,500 to validate on
> one sanitized workflow, that is useful qualification data and we should not
> pretend a free pilot will fix it.

**Acceptable movement:** signed $4,500 sprint, or the one-week $2,500 paid
assessment only when budget is the sole blocker and the assessment has a named
buyer and workflow.

**Do not offer:** free implementation, unpaid proof of concept, speculative
integration work, or unlimited access.

## “Let’s partner first and find customers later.”

> We are open to ecosystem alignment, but our design-partner program is for a
> named funded workflow. We can separate the two: continue the partnership
> discussion while identifying either your internal budget owner or one customer
> that can sponsor the paid sprint now.

An integration, referral promise, marketplace listing, or memorandum of
understanding does not count as the paid design partnership.

## “We need to see more proof before paying.”

> The sprint is the bounded proof mechanism. It uses synthetic or sanitized
> material, has written success criteria, and limits the commitment to two
> weeks. Which specific risk remains after those controls: technical feasibility,
> security, procurement, or budget?

- **Technical feasibility:** show the public implementation and verified
  commercial one-pager; scope one narrow event path.
- **Security:** use synthetic data and no production credentials.
- **Procurement:** identify the required vendor and purchase-order steps now.
- **Budget:** ask who owns the problem and whether $4,500 is within approval
  authority.

## “We already have logs, traces, or an audit trail.”

> Those show that events occurred. The question is whether you can reproduce the
> exact source, permission, policy, model, and memory state that authorized an
> older action after those inputs change—and see every downstream dependency
> before a record is corrected or deleted. If your current system already proves
> that end to end, Lians is duplicative and we should stop.

Ask the prospect to demonstrate one historical reconstruction. Do not claim a
gap without testing it.

## “We do not have budget right now.”

> Understood. Is the problem unfunded because it is not a current priority, or
> is there a different owner whose budget covers auditability, AI platform,
> model risk, compliance, or incident response?

If no current owner or budget exists, record “not now,” agree on a concrete
revisit trigger, and stop selling. Do not convert the engagement to free work.

## “Can you reduce the price?”

> The $4,500 scope is already constrained to one sanitized workflow and two
> weeks. We can reduce scope—not make the same work free. If $4,500 is just
> outside current authority, the fallback is a one-week $2,500 paid assessment
> with a narrower report and no implementation.

Do not introduce the fallback before the buyer confirms that budget is the only
remaining blocker.

## “Send information and we’ll circle back.”

> I’ll send the one-page scope. Before we end, who will review it, what decision
> will they make, and by what date? If no one owns the decision, we should call
> this exploratory rather than an active paid opportunity.

## “We can introduce you to customers.”

> That can be valuable. Please choose one customer with a consequential AI
> workflow and make the introduction while the context is fresh. We will qualify
> the customer directly and keep the $4,500 paid terms explicit.

Do not count the introduction itself as revenue or a design-partnership win.

## Final close

> We have a named workflow, success criterion, and two-week scope. The fee is
> $4,500, with $2,250 due before kickoff. Are you the approver? If yes, let’s
> complete the order-form owner, dates, and billing fields now. If not, who must
> join the decision and when?

## Walk-away test

Disqualify or downgrade the opportunity when the prospect will not provide:

1. one consequential workflow;
2. a person who owns the outcome;
3. a budget path;
4. a dated commercial decision;
5. willingness to sign and pay before implementation.
