# Lians 10 out of 10 pressure test

Date: 2026-08-21

## Verdict

No idea tested is 10 out of 10.

Calling one 10 out of 10 before Lians has real users and real outcomes would
hide the risks instead of removing them. A research score cannot substitute for
customer behavior.

The strongest candidate worth a live test is:

> **Refund.**

> Forward the receipt or support thread. Lians gets your money back.

This is not permission to rebuild the product. It is permission to run the
smallest free demand experiment.

## The criteria

A strong Lians company must:

1. solve a painful moment that already occurs in the real world;
2. work for a broad population through a specific first wedge;
3. create the result instead of advising or checking;
4. be simpler to explain than a general AI workflow;
5. survive the ChatGPT, Claude, Codex, and incumbent replacement test;
6. support repeat use;
7. produce a shareable outcome;
8. scale with declining human work;
9. support strong economics later without charging before value; and
10. have direct Lians evidence.

The tenth criterion forces every untested idea below 10 today.

## What current evidence says

- Returns are large and common. The National Retail Federation estimated that
  15.8 percent of annual retail sales, or $849.9 billion, would be returned in
  2025. It estimated a 19.3 percent online return rate. See the [2025 NRF
  returns report](https://nrf.com/media-center/press-releases/consumers-expected-to-return-nearly-850-billion-in-merchandise-in-2025).
- Consumers have used action products at meaningful scale. Chargeback says its
  agent canceled more than 400,000 unwanted subscriptions before the company
  joined Flex. See the [Chargeback announcement](https://www.joinchargeback.com/).
- The exact broad money-recovery idea already has competition. Lumo writes
  emails, reads replies, chases refunds, verifies outcomes, requires approval,
  and charges 10 percent only after recovery in the UK. See
  [Lumo](https://lumoapp.org/).
- The wider direction is real, but difficult. The UK Competition and Markets
  Authority says most consumer AI still supports decisions while coordination,
  monitoring, and action remain with the user. It identifies delegated outcomes
  as the larger opportunity. See its [2026 consumer agent
  analysis](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/agentic-ai-and-consumers/agentic-ai-and-consumers).
- Generic software creation is not an open wedge. OpenAI documents a Codex flow
  from a rough idea or repository through checks, deployment, and a live URL.
  See the [official Codex deployment use
  case](https://learn.chatgpt.com/use-cases/deploy-app-or-website).

This evidence proves categories and substitutes. It does not prove that users
want Lians.

## Why the other candidates fail

### Ship

It solves a real production problem but only for AI app builders. General
agents and hosting products continue moving into the same workflow. Arbitrary
production rescue also becomes an engineering service quickly.

### Sell

The result is clear, but the product is crowded and dependent on marketplaces
that own buyer demand, fees, accounts, and seller rules. Crosslist already
publishes one listing across more than eleven marketplaces and auto-removes it
after sale. See [Crosslist](https://crosslist.com/features/create-listings-with-ai).

### Return

Returns are universal, but many require packing, printing, pickup, drop-off, or
inspection. Software cannot honestly promise the finished result while the user
still owns the physical work.

### Appeal

Health insurance denials are dire, but the use case is regulated,
high-consequence, infrequent for most people, and already served by specialized
free products. Counterforce Health says it has handled more than 10,000 appeals.
See [Counterforce Health](https://www.counterforcehealth.tech/).

### Call

Calling businesses is broad and annoying, but major operating-system and model
providers are natural owners of voice agents. Calls also create consent,
identity, recording, reliability, and low shareability problems.

### Claim unclaimed money

NAUPA says about one in seven people has unclaimed property, but official state
search and claim processes are already free. The task is valuable but rarely
repeatable. See [NAUPA](https://unclaimed.org/what-is-unclaimed-property/).

## Why Refund is the closest candidate

### One word

> **Refund.**

### One trigger

> You were charged and did not get what you paid for.

### One input

> Forward the receipt or support thread.

### One finished result

> The money lands back in your account.

### Why it is more than a chatbot

ChatGPT can write one message. Lians must:

1. construct the factual evidence file;
2. check whether the case fits a supported rule;
3. identify the correct merchant channel;
4. ask for approval;
5. send from the user's authorized address;
6. read and classify replies;
7. request missing evidence;
8. follow deadlines and safe escalation rules; and
9. verify that the money returned.

The result is not prose. The result is a confirmed refund.

### Why it fits Lians

The existing thesis is that a person forwards something and Lians handles it
with human approval. Refund turns that mechanism into a measurable consumer
outcome. It can also reuse Lians Check principles for evidence, approval,
freshness, and fail-closed completion.

## Why Refund is still not 10 out of 10

1. Lians has recovered no money for a real user.
2. Lumo already demonstrates almost the exact broad product in the UK.
3. Merchant policies, forms, identities, and escalation paths vary.
4. False or exaggerated claims create fraud and trust risk.
5. Some cases require a package, phone call, bank dispute, or legal judgment.
6. Low-dollar refunds can cost more to pursue than they return.
7. Verifying a posted refund eventually requires reliable financial evidence.
8. General agents will keep adding external actions.

This is a strong experiment, not a defensible company yet.

## Exact first experiment

Recruit twenty people with a real U.S. merchant case worth at least $25. Accept
only:

- duplicate charges;
- canceled orders that were still charged;
- missing orders covered by written merchant policy;
- promised refunds that are overdue; and
- approved refunds that never posted.

The user forwards the evidence, approves the first message, and spends no more
than three minutes on normal intake. Run the work manually behind a simple
Lians case flow before building merchant integrations.

### Pass gate

- fifteen people connect a real receipt or support thread;
- twelve approve an external request;
- eight refunds land within fourteen days;
- confirmed refunds exceed $1,000;
- median user time is under three minutes;
- median human operating time falls below fifteen minutes for the last five
  cases;
- no false claim, unauthorized message, or material data incident occurs;
- four qualified referrals occur; and
- four users submit a second valid case within thirty days.

### Kill gate

Stop or narrow the idea if:

- people like the idea but will not forward a real case;
- one ordinary AI-written email resolves the same cases with equal effort;
- most cases require physical or regulated action;
- merchant exceptions keep human operating time flat;
- case values cannot support later unit economics;
- users do not return with another case; or
- no one refers a second user after receiving money.

## First 72 hours

### Hours 0 to 12

- publish one plain intake page for the free experiment;
- create the forwarding address and case consent language;
- define supported and rejected case types;
- prepare the approval and evidence record; and
- recruit the first five people with live cases.

### Hours 12 to 36

- work the first five cases manually;
- record every user minute and operator minute;
- run the shortest ordinary ChatGPT or Claude baseline for comparison;
- classify every merchant block and missing fact; and
- show no public success claim before money lands.

### Hours 36 to 72

- recruit fifteen more live cases from referrals and direct outreach;
- publish privacy-safe refund receipts only with user consent;
- build only the repeated step that consumed the most time;
- write the first merchant-specific use-case page from a completed case; and
- make a continue, narrow, or stop decision from the gate.

## Metrics

- visitor to real case submitted;
- case submitted to evidence connected;
- evidence connected to first action approved;
- approved to money refunded;
- dollars submitted and dollars recovered;
- time to refund;
- user minutes per case;
- human operating minutes per case;
- false or unsupported claim rate;
- second valid case within thirty days;
- referred valid case; and
- percentage of cases handled by a reusable resolution pattern.

The activation metric is **money confirmed back in the user's account**.

The scalability metric is **human operating time falling while confirmed
refunds rise**.

The assumptions, two-month scenarios, operator limits, unit economics, and
day-60 decision gates are defined in the [Refund scalability and 60-day
test](refund-scalability-and-60-day-test.md).

## Final decision

Do not rebuild Lians around Refund tonight.

Run the twenty-case free experiment first. If users hand over real cases, money
comes back, human work declines, and referrals appear, then build the narrow
workflow. If those things do not happen, the honest score stays below 10 and we
keep searching.
